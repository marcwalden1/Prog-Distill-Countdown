# Qwen3-0.6B-Base GRPO lr=1e-6: why the second epoch adds little (2026-10-11)

Runs (balanced-simple, kl=3e-3, rollout.n=4, seed1, constant LR, 1024-token cap):
- 1 ep: `Qwen3-0.6B-Base/balanced-simple-grpo-lr1e-6-kl3e-3-seed1` (1633 steps, log 49112081)
- 2 ep: `Qwen3-0.6B-Base/balanced-simple-grpo-lr1e-6-kl3e-3-ep2-seed1` (3266 steps, log 49629966)

Figures: `figures/Qwen3-0.6B-Base/Qwen3-0.6B-epochs/{id_vs_ood,pass1_vs_pass32}.png`
(`plot_qwen3_size_comparison.py --preset ep-0.6B`), and the 2-ep run in
`figures/Qwen3-base-grpo/id_vs_ood_all.png`.

## Headline

Full-set pass@1 / pass@32 (`analysis_data/idood_qwen3_base_grpo.csv`):

| | 1 ep @1600 | 2 ep @1600 | 2 ep @3200 |
|---|---|---|---|
| n=3,4 | 0.669 / 0.886 | 0.724 / 0.928 | 0.731 / 0.909 |
| n=5 | 0.190 / 0.481 | 0.217 / 0.574 | 0.213 / 0.511 |
| n=6 | 0.039 / 0.180 | 0.044 / 0.232 | 0.036 / 0.169 |

Epoch 2 gives ~0 on pass@1 and loses pass@32, mostly OOD.

## 1. The 1-ep vs 2-ep gap at step 1600 is run-to-run noise

The two runs saw identical prompts in identical order through step 1633 (checked
from the per-step rollout dumps `<ckpt dir>/<step>.jsonl`), same seed, constant
LR (`warmup_style: constant`), so nothing about the epoch count differs before
step 1633. The divergence (2-ep ahead from ~step 800, +0.055 in-dist pass@1 at
1600) comes from sampling nondeterminism. **Seed-level noise is about as large as
the effect of the whole second epoch** -- don't read either without more seeds.

## 2. Failures are almost entirely truncation at the 1024-token cap

Eval (temp 0.6, 32 samples, 150 random prompts/set; "trunc" = response >= 1020 tokens):

| | set | pass@1 | trunc | pass@1 if not truncated |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 ep @1600 | n=3,4 | 0.734 | 0.280 | 0.984 |
| 2 ep @1600 | n=5 | 0.234 | 0.763 | 0.899 |
| 2 ep @1600 | n=6 | 0.043 | 0.938 | 0.664 |
| 2 ep @3200 | n=3,4 | 0.736 | 0.247 | 0.939 |
| 2 ep @3200 | n=5 | 0.201 | 0.706 | 0.630 |
| 2 ep @3200 | n=6 | 0.024 | 0.856 | 0.166 |

Training rollouts (temp 1.0), 2-ep run: 26-31% truncated; 73-94% of non-correct
rollouts are truncated; only ~9% of truncated rollouts score 1.0. Almost every
response (~99%) opens with a guessed `<answer>` and then runs a trial-and-error
enumeration of expressions; when the enumeration doesn't hit within 1024 tokens
the response is cut off. That cut is what fails, not wrong arithmetic: an
in-dist response that finishes is right 94-98% of the time.

## 3. What epoch 2 actually changed: earlier stopping, not better search

From 1600 to 3200 the truncation rate drops on every set (n=6: 0.94 -> 0.86), but
the responses that now finish are mostly wrong OOD (pass@1 among finished: n=5
0.90 -> 0.63, n=6 0.66 -> 0.17). The model learned to commit sooner, which buys
nothing on n=3,4 (already short enough) and converts OOD truncations into
finished-but-wrong answers. pass@32 falls because the stopped searches lose the
long tail that occasionally found the answer.

## 4. Why the training signal stalls

Training reward plateaus at ~0.70-0.76 from ~step 1400 to 3266 (200-step means).
Zero-advantage groups (all 4 rollouts equal score) stay at 62-69% throughout
epoch 2: ~50% of groups all-correct, ~13-17% all-wrong (mostly all-truncated).
Only ~1/3 of each batch carries gradient, and the remaining errors are mostly
length-cap failures on hard-for-enumeration prompts, which GRPO with a binary
reward and 4 samples rarely gets a contrast on. Epoch 2 re-shuffles the same
362k prompts, so it brings no new kind of signal either.

## Not verified / next checks

- Single seed. A second seed of each would size the noise floor in section 1.
- Whether truncated searches would have succeeded given room: re-eval the 2-ep
  step-3200 checkpoint with a larger cap (e.g. 4096) on n=5/n=6. (The analogous
  Qwen2.5-0.5B check on 2026-06-09 found no truncation effect; this model's
  behaviour is different -- 70-94% OOD truncation.)
- Training with a larger `MAX_LENGTH` is the direct test of whether the cap is
  what stalls epoch 2.
